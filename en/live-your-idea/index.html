PROGRAMIGRANJE · KRAGUJEVAC · 2017

Live Your Idea

From curiosity to a first program.

ProgramiGranje is an idea by Miloš Milosavljević, an IT teacher from Kragujevac: helping children discover programming and robotics through play, experiments and hands-on work. It received development support through the “Live Your Idea” competition organised by SBB and the SBB Foundation.

A computer as a tool for creating

What if a child could make a game instead of simply playing one? Or write a program and watch a robot follow its instructions? These questions inspired ProgramiGranje. Its aim was to introduce primary school children to the creative side of technology and develop logical thinking, patience and independent problem-solving.

The workshops were designed as an accessible, practical complement to school learning. Free participation was intended to welcome children regardless of their families’ financial circumstances, while small groups allowed more time for questions, experiments and individual guidance.

Learning through experimentation

01

Scratch and first programs

Creating animations, games and simple programs with visual programming. An idea becomes a sequence of steps, with the result immediately visible on screen.

02

Robotics

Connecting code to a robot’s movement: testing commands, observing results and improving solutions through practical tasks.

03

Electronics and controllers

The programme planned to introduce Arduino controllers and Raspberry Pi computers, using sensors, motors and LEDs to connect computing, electronics and mechatronics.

2017

Selected from 580 ideas

In July 2017, ProgramiGranje was named one of four winning ideas in the “Live Your Idea” competition. The organisers received 580 applications from more than 70 places across Serbia. Selection opened the way for SBB Foundation support to implement the workshops.

The people and support behind the idea

The idea was conceived and led by Miloš Milosavljević, a teacher with years of experience working with children. Radoje Domanović Primary School provided a digital classroom with 25 computers, a projector and an interactive whiteboard. Parents and the school also helped obtain the initial equipment.

The project application emphasised expert instruction, suitable equipment and learning adapted to each child. The equipment was intended to benefit future groups too. The longer-term ambition was to expand the programme and connect with other communities.
